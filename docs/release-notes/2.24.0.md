## MeetingIntro is now a verified publisher

Connecting a Microsoft 365 calendar used to show a consent screen headed **"MeetingIntro — unverified"**, with a warning that nobody had vouched for the app. For anyone using a work account that was the end of the road: IT looks at an unverified multitenant app asking for calendar access and says no, and they are right to.

That screen now carries a blue **verified** badge and the publisher name **Stern & Signal Labs Limited**, backed by Microsoft's own check of the company's legal identity, its domain, and the person behind it. Nothing about what the app reads has changed — it is the same calendar permissions as before — but an administrator now has something they can actually approve.

### You will need to sign in to Microsoft once more

Getting verified meant registering the app afresh, and Microsoft ties a saved sign-in to the registration that issued it. Yours can't carry over.

So the first time this version talks to your calendar it will sign you out of Microsoft and say exactly why:

> MeetingIntro's Microsoft registration changed in this update, so your previous sign-in no longer works. Sign in once more and it will stay signed in.

Settings → Calendar → Sign In, once. Everything else — which calendars you monitor, your reminders, armed auto-joins, RSVPs you've already sent — is untouched. This is a one-off; it won't happen on later updates.

Google Calendar, iCloud, and any calendar you reach through macOS Calendar are unaffected and stay signed in.

### Still true, and worth repeating

Work tenants under Microsoft's managed consent policy continue to need an administrator's approval for calendar access. Verification doesn't remove that step — it's what makes the request a reasonable one to send. Settings → Calendar still gives you a copyable approval link to pass to IT.
