### New

**New Event has been redesigned.** Typing is still the whole interface, but what it produces is now one card you can read at a glance and correct without retyping.

- **Modifier phrases become chips.** Type *all day for Labour Day* and the field keeps **Labour Day** while an **All day** chip sits beside it. The phrase is a setting, not part of the name, so it no longer ends up in the event's title. Click the chip's × to drop it.
- **One preview card** carries the title, when it is, the meeting link and the calendar, with **Edit** for everything else.
- **Assumptions and conflicts collapse into one counted box.** Instead of a stack of orange labels, you get *"2 things to check"* with the reasons in plain language and buttons to **Pick a date** or **View conflict**.

**A Details screen for the rest.** Edit opens title, starts and ends with an all-day toggle, **repeat**, calendar, meeting link, guests and notes. ⌘↩ creates from there.

**Repeating events.** Never, Daily, Weekly on the weekday, Monthly on the date, or Yearly. Every one of them is written to macOS Calendar and to Microsoft 365 identically, which is why the list is short: a repeat option that one of the two would quietly drop is worse than no option at all.

**Choose the calendar for a single event.** The New Event form now has a calendar row listing every calendar you can write to across both sources. Picking one also decides where the event is created, so choosing an Outlook calendar writes to Outlook and sends the invitations, without changing the app-wide setting in Settings → Calendar.

### Fixes

**The overlay for simultaneous meetings could appear as an empty dark panel.** When two meetings started together on a screen shorter than about 1100 points, the panel was sized from a measurement taken at the wrong width — the height described a layout that never happened, the window and its contents disagreed, and the result was a panel with nothing drawn in it that sat there until dismissed. It is now measured at the width it is actually shown at, the same way the single-meeting overlay always was, and the contents are pinned to the window so they cannot lay out beyond the visible area. If a panel does end up shorter than its contents, the log now says so with both numbers.

**The diagnostic log now records the size of every overlay panel**, not just the single-meeting one. The blank panel above was reported with no dimensions in the log at all, which is half the reason it took a screenshot to find.
