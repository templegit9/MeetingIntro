Four things you asked for, and one you didn't.

### Copy a meeting without opening your calendar

Every meeting row's **⋯** menu now has **Copy Join Link**, **Copy Details** and **Copy Notes**. Three separate actions rather than one "Copy", because they're different jobs: the link is what you paste into a chat, the details are a formatted block someone can read, and the notes are the organiser's own text — where the agenda and the dial-in number actually live. Actions that have nothing to copy don't appear at all.

### A moved meeting no longer disappears

Cancellations have survived a relaunch since v2.2.0. Reschedules hadn't — a meeting moved while you were away from your desk fired one notification and left no trace, and if you missed the banner you missed the move. They now get the same treatment: an orange notice in the dropdown that stays until you dismiss it, and is still there after a restart.

### Meeting status in the menu bar — off by default

Settings → Menu Bar → **Meeting Status** adds a **second** menu bar item showing the next meeting as it approaches, turning orange once one has started and you haven't joined.

**Your app icon never changes.** It stays a steady clock so it's always easy to find — that was a deliberate decision and this doesn't undo it. The new item appears only when there's something to say and disappears afterwards.

One honest limit: "late" means the app hasn't seen you join. Open the link from Calendar.app or dial in from your phone and it can't tell, and will still say late. That's why it's bounded to a few minutes and off until you turn it on.

### The full calendar, without leaving the menu bar

**⌘⇧C**, or the expand control beside Refresh, grows the dropdown into a month, week or day view with an agenda rail alongside. **Esc** collapses it. It loads 45 days ahead when you open it, so it shows more than the dropdown's usual week — but it can only look forward, so earlier days in the current month come back empty.

### And the small one

The in-app updater used to tell you to run `brew upgrade --cask meetingintro` when it failed — missing the tap, and useless if the app wasn't installed through Homebrew at all. It now names the full cask and, when Homebrew has no record of the app, points you at the download instead of a command that can never work.
