Invitations now have a place to be answered.

### Accept, decline, or propose a different time

Invitations awaiting a reply appear at the top of the dropdown, as cards carrying only what changes an answer: who asked, when it is, how far out when that matters, and a conflict line when there is one. Two at a time, the rest behind a count — it's a prompt to answer, not a second calendar.

**Propose** suggests one slot rather than handing you a date picker, because the reason people don't counter-propose is the work of finding a time. It offers the soonest gap of the same length on your calendar, inside your working hours, never at a weekend — and the card says plainly that it hasn't checked anyone else's availability, because it hasn't. You can pick your own time instead, without leaving the dropdown.

**Declining asks once, and names the person who'll be told.** Accepting and tentative send straight through; only the answer that disappoints someone gets a second beat.

### It tells you when a reply didn't send

Previously every response was fire-and-forget: a decline that never reached the organizer looked exactly like one that did. Now a reply that fails says so, and says what it means — the organizer wasn't notified — with a Retry beside it. An answer you just gave lingers for a moment so you can take it back.

### What it can't do, and says so

Replying to invitations only works for Microsoft 365. Apple gives apps no way to RSVP to an iCloud or Google invitation, so those are still listed — you need to know they're waiting — but they offer **Open in Calendar** rather than buttons that would do nothing. Likewise, proposing a new time needs the organizer to have allowed it; when they haven't, the option simply isn't there.

### Also

Working hours for the suggested slot are in Settings → Calendar → Invitations.
