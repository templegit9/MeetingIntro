Five fixes, all found by reading one user's diagnostic log from a single day.

### Fixed: a calendar mirror that could never recover

If you edited a mirror to point at different calendars, the safety brake — which stops a sync that would delete an unusual number of copies — could trip and then refuse to let go. Re-saving cleared it, the next sync hit the same condition and tripped it again, and the mirror silently did nothing no matter how many times you tried. In the log that showed up as a mirror wanting to remove 80 of 98 copies, sixteen refusals in eighty seconds, and seven Re-enable clicks inside one second.

Changing which calendars a mirror covers now counts as saying you meant it, so the cleanup goes through. The button says what it does — **"Re-enable and sync"** — and the message tells you exactly how many copies will be removed before you press it. The brake still protects the case it was built for, a source calendar that briefly returns nothing, and renaming a mirror still authorises nothing.

### Fixed: half a meeting row sliced against the menu

The dropdown's meeting list could cut a row through the middle at its bottom edge, with the New Event row sitting right on the cut, which read as a rendering glitch rather than "there's more below". With **Fit dropdown height to content** on, the list now sizes to a whole number of rows so the edge always falls between them. With it off, a soft fade makes a partly visible row read as continuing.

### Fixed: the camera cover reminder giving up too easily

If a check landed while a meeting was still running or the camera was still in use, the nudge was thrown away. Both of those are temporary, so it now waits and tries again for about ten minutes — which matters most on a day of back-to-back meetings, exactly when you're most likely to forget the cover.

### Under the hood

**A log you can trust.** A reminder's notification and voice path could re-run about ten times for the same meeting at the same threshold. You never saw ten notifications, but the app redid the work each time and the diagnostic log claimed ten reminders where one had fired — misleading precisely when you're trying to work out why something did or didn't happen.

**No more refresh storms.** Every edit in Calendar.app, and every write a mirror made, told the app to re-read your calendar immediately; on a busy day that meant dozens of extra full refreshes, each one re-running reminders and mirror syncing behind it. Those bursts now collapse into a single refresh. Granting calendar access still updates the app right away.
